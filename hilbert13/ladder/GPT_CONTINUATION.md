## 1. The orbit bound and the genus-336 table

A pencil means a rational subfield C(f), equivalently a map to P1 up to target
automorphism. Its stabiliser K acts on its target. The kernel K0 fixes f, so
|K0| divides the degree m; K/K0 is a finite subgroup of PGL2(C).

The maximal proper subgroups of A7 have orders 360, 168, 120, 72, and types
A6, PSL2(7), S5, (A4 x C3):2. The first two are simple and do not embed in
PGL2(C). S5 has only A5 and itself as nontrivial normal kernels; their orders
are incompatible with m<60. For the order-72 case any permissible target
quotient requires 3|m. For example, quotienting its normal C3 gives S4.
Thus |K|<=72, and |K|<72 if 3 does not divide m. The next possible subgroup
order is at most 60. Consequently an orbit has at least 35 members, and at
least 42 when 3 does not divide m.

Let the compositum of the orbit subfields be F. It is A7-stable. Simplicity
implies the action on F is faithful unless F is pointwise fixed; the latter
would force 2520|m. If [C(C):F]=e>=2 and m<=24, the orbit functions on the
curve with function field F have degree m/e<=12. Farb--Wolfson Lemma 2.2
gives genus at most (m/e-1)^2<=121, contrary to the minimum faithful A7 genus
136. Therefore the orbit subfields generate C(C), for every m<=24.

There is a detail in obtaining a birational pair. A nonbirational pair does
not necessarily become birational after adding just one arbitrarily chosen
orbit function. Instead, extend its compositum to a maximal proper field
generated by a subset of the orbit functions. Adding any orbit function
outside that field then generates C(C). If the maximal field has index e,
its genus is at most (m/e-1)^2, and Castelnuovo--Severi gives

    g(C) <= e(m/e-1)^2 + (e-1)(m-1)
         = m^2/e + em - 3m + 1.

Here e|m and 2<=e<=m/2, since the field contains two distinct pencils.
The argument and Farb--Wolfson's lemma have no degree-17 cutoff.

Claude's exact table is reproduced by `weighted_budget_checks.py`:

|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 20 | 181 | 228 | 25 | 90 / 120 / 135 |
| 21 | 148 | 253 | 64 | 100 / 133 / 150 |
| 22 | 221 | 279 | 105 | 110 / 147 / 165 |
| 23 | none | 306 | 148 | 121 / 161 / 181 |
| 24 | 265 | 335 | 193 | 132 / 176 / 198 |

The pi_1 theorem applies in P7 (Petrakiev, Theorem 2.7(a), n>=4). The
pi_2 theorem does **not** automatically apply in P7: Theorem 2.16(a) requires
n>=8. Section 3 below uses it in P8, where the range is valid.

For a birational pair, its image B in Q=P1 x P1 has type (m,m). A dependent
third is the ratio of two coprime (1,1) forms. Its base scheme has length two;
after resolution the centres are either two proper points or one proper point
and one infinitely near point. Its successive B-multiplicities r,s satisfy
r+s=m, and its cost in the genus defect is binom(r,2)+binom(s,2).
Distinct third pencils have distinct **base clusters**, including tangent
directions in the infinitely near case. Two centres impose two independent
linear conditions on the four-dimensional space of (1,1) forms, leaving
exactly the two-dimensional pencil. Merely recording their support is not
enough. Resolving the union of all clusters makes simultaneous costs additive.

## 2. A weighted base-point invariant

Let an integral surface in X=(P1)^3 have multidegree (a,b,1), a,b>=1.
It is the graph of a coprime pencil in |O_Q(a,b)|. Resolve every base point,
including infinitely near ones. In the total-transform basis its fibre class
is

    D = a H1 + b H2 - sum n_i E_i,    n_i>=1.

Since D is a fibre, D^2=0, hence sum n_i^2=2ab. Suppose a curve on this
surface has all three coordinate degrees m and its projection to Q is
birational. Write r_i>=0 for its successive multiplicities in this resolution.
Then

    T := sum n_i r_i = (a+b-1)m,
    (m-1)^2 - g >= sum binom(r_i,2).

The first equality is intersection with D. The second follows by the
arithmetic-genus drop on each blowup, with any remaining singularity defect
nonnegative. Cauchy--Schwarz and n_i>=1 give

    sum r_i^2 >= T^2/(2ab),    sum r_i <= T.

Consequently

    g <= (m-1)^2 - T^2/(4ab) + T/2.                 (WB)

No smoothness assumption on the graph surface, no simple-base-point assumption,
and no omission of infinitely near points is made. Two particularly useful
specialisations are

    (2,1,1):  g <= m^2/2 - m + 1,
    (2,2,1):  g <= 7m^2/16 - m/2 + 1.

The invariant is the prescribed weighted incidence T against the available
squared multiplicity 2ab. It converts the geometry of a third pencil into a
lower bound for the singularity defect of the first two.

### The (2,2,2) case: an anticanonical normalization lemma

**Lemma.** If S is an integral projective Gorenstein surface with omega_S
isomorphic to O_S, the minimal resolution Y of its normalization has a
canonical divisor K_Y<=0.

Choose the rational two-form given by a dualizing generator on S. Finite
duality for normalization identifies its dualizing module with the conductor
times this generator. At codimension-one points of the normalization the
divisor of the form is therefore nonpositive. This statement uses Weil
divisors, not a Q-Cartier assumption on the normalization.

On Y write the divisor of the form as K_Y=A-N, where A,N are effective with
disjoint components. Every component of A is exceptional. For an irreducible
exceptional curve E on the minimal resolution,

    K_Y.E = 2p_a(E)-2-E^2 >= 0.

Indeed E^2 is a negative integer; the only potential exception is a smooth
rational (-1) curve, excluded by minimality. If A were nonzero, negative
definiteness of the exceptional intersection matrix would give

    0 <= K_Y.A = A^2-N.A < 0,

a contradiction. Thus A=0.

An integral (2,2,2) surface in X has trivial dualizing sheaf by adjunction.
Put H=O_X(1,1,1). Its singular locus cannot contain a curve of H-degree
greater than 24. To see this, write its equation as q. Some global vector
field v on X has v(q) not proportional to q: otherwise Cq would be an
invariant line in the nontrivial irreducible SL2^3 representation
Sym^2(C^2) tensor Sym^2(C^2) tensor Sym^2(C^2). Every singular point of S
lies in {q=v(q)=0}, a complete-intersection curve of degree (2H)^2.H=24.

For a curve B of degree d>24 on S, its strict transform C on Y is birational
to B and is not a component of the negative canonical divisor. The latter's
components map into the conductor or singular locus. The pullback of H is
nef and big with H^2=12. Adjunction and Hodge index give

    g(B) <= p_a(C) <= 1+C^2/2 <= 1+d^2/24.

For balanced degree m>=9, d=3m, so

    (2,2,2):  g <= 1+3m^2/8.                       (K0)

This includes nonnormal surfaces and singular normalizations. At m=24 the
three surface bounds (2,1,1), (2,2,1), (2,2,2) are 265, 241, 217.

## 3. The sharp independent-triple theorem

**Theorem.** Let C be a smooth complex curve with three degree-m pencils,
m>=9, every pair birational. Suppose the eight products of their two basis
sections are linearly independent. Then

    g(C) <= floor(m^2/2-m+1).                      (IT)

Write A(m)=m^2/2-m+1, L=L1 L2 L3, and suppose g>A(m). The Segre map gives
a birational, nondegenerate degree-3m image B in P7, contained in X.

First h0(C,L)=8. Otherwise adjoining a ninth section gives a birational
degree-3m model in P8. Direct evaluation gives pi_2(3m,8)<=A(m) for m>=9.
For m=3k+r, r=0,1,2, the difference A(m)-pi_2(3m,8) is (k+r)/2.
Petrakiev's Theorem 2.16(a), in its stated range n=8 and 3m>=19, puts that
model on an integral surface of degree <=8. Project this surface to P7.
Its image cannot be a curve: a curve image would have degree <=8, whereas
B has degree 3m>8. Thus the projected image is a surface of degree <=8.

Each Segre quadric contains B. If it did not contain the projected surface,
its intersection with that surface would have degree <=16<3m. Hence the
surface is contained in X. An integral surface in X has divisor type (a,b,c)
and degree 2(a+b+c). None of a,b,c can vanish: a zero coefficient would
force the birational (m,m) pair-image into a fixed curve of one of those
small bidegrees. Thus only (1,1,1) or a permutation of (2,1,1) is possible.
The first is a hyperplane section, contrary to the eight independent
products. The second contradicts (WB). This proves h0(C,L)=8.

The image B is linearly normal, since its eight ambient sections already
exhaust the sections on its normalization. For a general hyperplane section
Gamma in P6, linear normality lifts all quadrics of Gamma, and

    h_B(2) = 8+h_Gamma(2).

If h_Gamma(2)<=18, then h_B(2)<=26, whereas h_X(2)=27. There is therefore
a quadric containing B whose restriction to X is nonzero. Its restriction
has multidegree (2,2,2). An irreducible surface component containing B has
each coefficient between 0 and 2. Again every coefficient is positive, so
it has type (1,1,1), (2,1,1), (2,2,1), or (2,2,2), up to permutation.
The first is excluded by nondegeneracy and the others by (WB) and (K0):
for m>=9 their bounds are all <=A(m).

It follows that h_Gamma(2)>=19. Uniform position gives

    h(i+j) >= min(3m,h(i)+h(j)-1),    h(1)=7.

Thus h(2j)>=min(3m,18j+1) and h(2j+1)>=min(3m,18j+7). Summing
the deficits 3m-h(i) bounds the arithmetic, hence geometric, genus.
For m=6k+r the resulting bounds are

| r | Hilbert-function genus bound |
|---|---|
| 0 | 18k^2-8k+1 |
| 1 | 18k^2-2k |
| 2 | 18k^2+4k |
| 3 | 18k^2+10k+2 |
| 4 | 18k^2+16k+5 |
| 5 | 18k^2+22k+8 |

Each is at most floor(A(m)), contradicting g>A(m). At m=24 this
Hilbert-function bound is 257 and pi_2(72,8)=261, both below 265.

The leading result is sharp for even m. A smooth (2,1,1) surface is a
degree-four del Pezzo surface. A general curve in |-kK| has balanced degrees
m=2k and genus 2k^2-2k+1=A(m). The two degree-one surface projections are
birational, and the remaining projection restricts birationally to a general
curve: that curve and its deck transform are distinct. The products are
independent because this curve is not contained in an ambient hyperplane
for k sufficiently large, in particular for the relevant m>=10.

## 4. The genus-266 A7 theorem

Suppose a faithful A7-curve has g>=266 and minimum pencil degree m<=24.
Its orbit subfields generate the full field, as above. Farb--Wolfson gives
g<=(m-1)^2, so 18<=m<=24. The completed nonbirational-pair argument has
bound at most 265 throughout these degrees; hence every orbit pair is
birational. By (IT), every third is dependent, since A(m)<=265.

Fix a pair and resolve simultaneously the base clusters of its at least
33 distinct dependent thirds. Let delta=(m-1)^2-g. Form the simple graph
whose vertices are the centres and whose edges are the two-centre clusters.
An edge with successive multiplicities r,s has r+s=m. Multiplicity zero
cannot occur here: binom(m,2)>delta. There are no repeated edges, since
the complete base cluster determines the pencil.

For every 18<=m<=24,

    delta <= (m-1)^2-266
          < 2 min_{1<=r<m} [binom(r,2)+binom(m-r,2)].

Therefore two disjoint edges are impossible. A simple graph with matching
number one is a star or a triangle. With at least 33 edges it is a star.
If its common centre has multiplicity r, its cost is at least

    binom(r,2)+33 binom(m-r,2).

Exact enumeration excludes m=18,...,23. At m=24 the only possibility is
r=23 and every leaf of multiplicity one. The common centre is a proper
point: an infinitely near centre would require an ancestor of multiplicity
at least 23, costing at least 2 binom(23,2)>delta.

Blow up that proper point P on Q. The (1,1)-E system maps birationally
to P2, and the strict transform of B maps to a plane curve of degree
2m-r=25. If its plane image had a singular point, projection from that
point would give a pencil of degree <=23, contradicting the minimum m=24.
It is therefore smooth, and has genus (25-1)(25-2)/2=276.

Every faithful A7-curve has genus congruent to 1 modulo 3. Indeed
Riemann--Hurwitz has terms 1260/e for the possible inertia orders
e=2,3,4,5,6,7, all divisible by 3, and its quotient-genus term is also
divisible by 3. But 276 is not congruent to 1 modulo 3. This contradiction
proves gon(C)>=25 for g>=266.

The same checks give algebraic lower bounds 18,20,21,22,23 at genera
136,169,199,211,241 respectively. The genus-136 borderline would be a
smooth plane curve of degree 18; Harui's |Aut|<=6d^2 bound rules out A7
(1944<2520). The existing certified spectral result is stronger there:
gon(C)>=24. These lesser algebraic bounds do not settle any of the seven
remaining spectral targets.

## 6. The degree-seven map and the two quotient models

Let s=1+/-4sqrt(21)/21, t=2s/3-10/21, so 21s^2-42s+5=0. Define

    q_s(z)=z^7/7-(s+1)z^6/6+(s+t)z^5/5-tz^4/4,
    r_s=-128(51s-65)/1750329,
    beta_s(z)=1-q_s(z)/r_s.

The derivative q_s'(z)=z^3(z-1)(z^2-sz+t). The two roots of the quadratic
have the common q-value r_s; q_s(0)=q_s(1)=0. The resulting passport is
[2^2 1^3, 4 2 1, 7]. Exact symbolic checks and a finite-field separation
witness are reproduced by `belyi_quotient_checks.py`.

The equal-critical-value parameter factorization is

    (7s^2-7s+4)(21s^2-56s+40)(21s^2-42s+5).

The middle factor degenerates the two quadratic critical points. The first
is the sqrt(-7) PSL2(7) case; the last gives A7. For the last, the unramified
specialisation modulo 17, s=3, beta_s(z)=2 has factor degrees 2 and 5.
Thus the arithmetic monodromy contains an element of order divisible by 5;
this excludes the PSL2(7) geometric group, whose normalizer in S7 has order
dividing 336. The other maximal proper subgroups cannot contain both the
7-cycle and the specified order-four element.

The discriminant is

    disc_z(beta_s(z)-T) = -7(-1/r_s)^6 T^2(T-1)^4.

The normalized degree-seven Belyi maps have definition field Q(sqrt(21)).
A common field for the regular splitting covers with their full marked A7
action is Q(sqrt(21),sqrt(-7)). There are two beta choices and two outer
labellings. This is not a claim about the minimal fields of definition of
the four *unmarked* underlying curves.

### Function-field model of the elliptic quotient

Make the sextic G_s(X,z)=(beta_s(X)-beta_s(z))/(X-z) monic, and let its
six roots be r1,...,r6. The geometric group over C(z) is the point stabiliser
A6. On six labels there are 15 perfect matchings (synthemes) and six
pentads: each pentad consists of five matchings partitioning the 15 edges.
For a matching M put sigma_M=sum_{ij in M} r_i r_j, and set

    u_Pi=sum_{M in Pi} sigma_M^3,
    R_E(U,z)=product_{six pentads Pi}(U-u_Pi).

These coefficients are symmetric functions of the roots, hence belong to
Q(sqrt(21))(z). The stabilizer of a pentad in S6 is the exotic S5; its
intersection with A6 is the transitive A5 in Claude's question. The
normalization of R_E=0 is the desired genus-one curve C/A5. The cube is
essential: the analogous sums of the first and second powers are constant.
This is an exact invariant-resolvent specification, not an expanded
Weierstrass equation or a computed point on that equation.

### Function-field model of the genus-two quotient

For each of the ten unordered partitions A|A^c into triples put

    v_A=product_{i in A}r_i+product_{i in A^c}r_i,
    R_S(V,z)=product_{ten partitions}(V-v_A).

The stabilizer in A6 is (S3 wr C2) intersect A6, of order 36 and type
3^2:4. The normalization of R_S=0 has genus two and is the Q1 quotient.

The actual family separates these invariants. At p=457, s=118, z=29,
beta_s(z)=173, the seven roots are [29,100,102,274,302,389,390]. The six
pentad values are [128,123,375,88,438,302], and the ten partition values
are [130,40,334,449,301,390,185,428,395,391], each list with distinct entries.
Thus the orbit sizes six and ten hold generically.

An integral form useful for future elimination is lambda=21s,
lambda^2-42lambda+105=0, Z=18z, and

    P_lambda(X)=X^7-(lambda+21)X^6+36(lambda-6)X^5-324(lambda-15)X^4.

The integral sextic (P_lambda(X)-P_lambda(Z))/(X-Z) can be used instead of
G_s. Modular interpolation plus CRT is a possible route to expanded

### What the isotypes do and do not prove

The 105 involution fixed divisors span the permutation module

    1+6+14_(4,3)+2*14_(5,2)+21+35.

The only overlap with H1(C) is the unique source copy of 21 and 35. Rational
Abel--Jacobi maps from those simple source modules are each zero or injective.
The Klein difference and the lift of the old condition (*) have nonzero
components in both source copies. Thus they are torsion together. The
degree-zero divisor supported at the branch points has Gram matrix 18I in
the involution-fixed-divisor basis; the Klein projections have squared norms
36/5,144/5,12,18,6 in the 6,14_(5,2),14_(4,3),21,35 parts respectively.

The A5 quotient's H1 detects the 21-part; the 3^2:4 quotient detects the
35-part. All 42 conjugates of the transitive A5 detect the Klein difference,
and 58 of the 70 conjugates of 3^2:4 do. One must choose a detecting conjugate
for the latter. Non-torsion of either pushed-forward class would settle both
questions. Neither an isotype calculation nor a nonzero reduction at one
finite prime establishes infinite order. To speak of P_E as a point of E
rather than Jac(E), an origin must be selected.

Manin--Drinfeld concerns cuspidal divisors on congruence modular curves. It
cannot be applied directly to elliptic-point divisors on this compact,
non-congruence triangle cover. Results for arbitrary finite-index modular
subgroups give torsion criteria and obstructions, rather than a blanket
torsion theorem; they do not resolve these two classes. There is also a concrete
counterexample: Banerjee--Merel, *The Heisenberg covering of the Fermat curve*,
Theorem 2, exhibit a cuspidal divisor of infinite order on the fifth Heisenberg
curve, a non-congruence modular curve. This refutes a blanket non-congruence
version for cusps; it does not determine the elliptic-point classes here.

## 7. Degree ten and criticality

Martens gives dimension at most seven for W^1_10 on this nonhyperelliptic
genus-64 quotient. The wrong-isotype residual condition lies in A^10 x E1^15,
an abelian variety of dimension 30+15=45. This dimension count alone does
not imply the particular fibre is empty. A map from a seven-dimensional
space into a 45-dimensional target cannot be transverse to a point *at an
intersection*. Proving that transversality for the fixed torsion translate
would already amount to proving emptiness; generic translates say nothing
about this particular class. A useful argument needs the actual differential
(evaluation of holomorphic forms at residual points, together with the
Petri tangent space) and control of singular components. No new algebraic
gon(D)>=11 theorem follows from the count. The existing spectral result
already gives gon(D)>=12.

Claude's frame argument is valid. A constant G-invariant sum of squares of
first eigenfunctions produces a harmonic map to a sphere with constant
positive energy. Its invariant holomorphic Hopf differential vanishes on a
triangle orbifold, making it a conformal minimal immersion whose induced
metric is a positive constant times the hyperbolic metric. Bryant excludes
even local minimal surfaces of constant negative curvature in a sphere.

For the variation argument one must use the full cone of invariant PSD forms
on E1, including any reducible eigenspace, rather than a single chosen frame.
The normalized cone is compact; no member has constant frame function.
Separating its mean-zero frame functions gives an invariant conformal
variation which raises every lowest eigenvalue branch. Hence the gain is
strict. This proves no percentage gain. The quoted 2.7% uses the numerical
baseline about .34627. Starting only with the conservative certificate about
.340892 requires about 4.30%, or an improved baseline certificate.

Ros explicitly records the broader nonextremality of hyperbolic metrics,
using Bryant. The precise triangle-orbifold invariant-conformal formulation
was not located verbatim. No priority claim is made. The identification of
the first eigenspace and a spectral gap beyond it remain separate numerical
inputs unless certified.

## 8. The genus-fourteen Hurwitz corollary

In PSL2(F13), an involution tau has centralizer of order 12, type D12. There
are three V4 subgroups through tau. Each normalizer is A4 of order 12, and
any two of these normalizers generate PSL2(F13), of order 1092. The exact
matrix-group computation is in `psl13_transport_check.py`.

On each genus-14 (2,3,7) Hurwitz curve, tau has six fixed points and the
quotient D has genus six. The good reflections on D have six fixed points
and generate an S3. If a pencil has degree d<=2, its saturated determinant
with its reflected pencil has degree 2d<6, forcing invariance under each
reflection; S3 invariance would force 6|d, a contradiction.

For d=3 the lift to the original curve has degree six. Since 4 does not
divide six, saturation forces the relevant Klein invariance. Averaging over
the order-12 centralizer and transporting between two A4 normalizers gives
a full-group invariant line bundle of degree 3*12*6=216. The order-13
subgroup acts freely, so 13 must divide its degree, contradiction. Thus
gon(D)>=4. The general genus-six upper bound is four, so gon(D)=4 for
all three Hurwitz curves. A search did not locate this exact corollary;
absence from the search is not a novelty proof.
