## 1. Exact quadratic systems

Let `V` be the exceptional six of `3.A7`, with its order-three central character. Put `S=Sym(V)`, the coordinate ring of `P(V*)`. The exact character calculation gives
\[
S_2=Q_6\oplus Q_{15},\qquad Q_6\cong V^*,
\tag{1}
\]
with both summands irreducible and nonisomorphic. Also `dim S_3^{3.A7}=1`.

**Lemma 1 [X].** Both systems of quadrics have empty projective base locus. More precisely,
\[
Q_6 S_5=S_7,\qquad Q_{15}S_2=S_4.
\tag{2}
\]

*Exact certificate.* For a section `g -> M_g` of the projective representation, the projector onto `Q6` is
\[
P_6=\frac6{2520}\sum_{g\in A_7}\operatorname{tr}(M_g)\,\operatorname{Sym}^2(M_g),
\qquad P_{15}=1-P_6.
\tag{3}
\]
Changing a lift by `omega` multiplies the trace by `omega` and its symmetric square by `omega^2`, leaving the summand unchanged. Formula (3) is the usual character projector for `V*`, since `\overline{chi_{V*}}=chi_V`.

The matrices have coefficients in `Z[omega]`; the only denominators in the projectors divide 2520. Specialize at `p=43` under each of `omega=6,36`. The projectors have ranks `6,15`. In the monomial bases, the multiplication matrices have the exact ranks

| quadratic system | degree | rank | dimension of the full homogeneous piece |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `Q6` | 7 | 792 | 792 |
| `Q15` | 3 | 55 | 56 |
| `Q15` | 4 | 126 | 126 |

The script also checks the dual representation. Every computation is modular integer arithmetic. A full-rank minor after specialization is nonzero in characteristic zero, proving (2). At a common projective zero, every polynomial in the corresponding homogeneous piece would vanish; the coordinate powers give a contradiction. QED.

**Theorem 2 (no quadrics).** Let `Z` be any nonempty closed subset of `P(V*)` stable under the projective `A7`-action. Then `I(Z)_2=0`.

*Proof.* Its degree-two ideal is a `3.A7`-subrepresentation of the multiplicity-free sum (1). If nonzero, it contains `Q6` or `Q15`. Then `Z` is contained in the empty base locus of that summand, by Lemma 1. QED.

Thus the restriction of quadrics to any nonempty invariant curve is injective, with image dimension exactly 21. This does not assert that `h0(2L60)=21`: projective normality is a separate question.

## 2. Jacobian and apolarity

Choose the unique invariant cubic
\[
0\ne F\in\operatorname{Sym}^3V,
\]
and the unique invariant dual cubic
\[
0\ne F^\vee\in\operatorname{Sym}^3V^*.
\]
Here elements of `Sym(V)` act as constant-coefficient differential operators on `Sym(V*)`; an *apolar ideal* is the ideal of operators annihilating a polynomial.

**Theorem 3.**

1. `Q6` is the span of the six first partial derivatives of `F`. Its ideal is a complete intersection of six quadrics, with Hilbert series `(1+t)^6` for the quotient. In particular the cubic hypersurface is smooth.
2. The ideal generated by `Q15` is exactly `Ann(F^vee)`. Its quotient has Hilbert function `(1,6,6,1)` and is Artinian Gorenstein, with socle degree 3.

*Proof.* Differentiation gives a nonzero equivariant map `V* -> S2`. Irreducibility makes it injective, and (1) identifies its image with `Q6`. Lemma 1 makes the six partial derivatives a homogeneous system of parameters. The polynomial ring is Cohen–Macaulay, so they form a regular sequence, giving the complete-intersection Hilbert series. Euler's identity shows that their empty projective zero set is equivalent to smoothness of `F=0`.

For the dual cubic, the second-derivative map `S2 -> V*` is nonzero and equivariant. It has rank six and kernel `Q15`, by (1). Thus `(Q15)` is contained in the apolar ideal. The degree-three ideal generated by `Q15` has no trivial constituent: a trivial summand in `Q15 tensor V` would require `Q15* = V`, which is impossible by dimension. Since `S3` contains a trivial constituent, its quotient has dimension at least one. The exact rank 55 in Lemma 1 bounds that dimension above by one. In degree four the quotient vanishes. Therefore its Hilbert function is `(1,6,6,1)`.

The apolar algebra of a nonzero cubic with six independent first partial derivatives has that same Hilbert function: derivative orders three, two, one, zero give dimensions `1,6,6,1`, and higher operators vanish. Equality of Hilbert functions proves `(Q15)=Ann(F^vee)`. The pairing induced by applying operators of complementary degrees to `F^vee` is perfect, proving the Gorenstein assertion. QED.

This identifies the two systems without a numerical equation for the curve. The complete intersection concerns the **Jacobian ideal of the ambient cubic**; it is not a claim that the degree-60 curve is a complete intersection.

## 3. The Klein divisors are exact

**Corollary 4.** On each degree-60 curve,
\[
\operatorname{div}(Q_H|_C)=5O_H,
\qquad 2L_{60}\sim5O_H.
\tag{4}
\]

*Proof.* Theorem 2 proves that the restriction is nonzero. Its zero divisor is `H`-invariant of degree 120. The `H`-orbits on the curve have sizes `24,42,84,168`, with a unique orbit of size 24. The only nonnegative solution of
\[
120=24a+42b+84c+168d
