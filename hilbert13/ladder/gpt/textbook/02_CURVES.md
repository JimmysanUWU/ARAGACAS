# 2. The alternating curves and ramification transport

## 2.1 Covers and fixed-point counts

A generating triple $(a,b,c)$ with $abc=1$ and respective orders $2,4,7$ determines a connected regular $A_7$-cover of $\mathbb P^1$. With $a=(12)(34)$ fixed, exact enumeration gives 96 generating choices of $b$. There are 10080 ordered triples, four inner conjugacy classes and two outer types: $\{0,1\}$ and $\{12,14\}$. Replacing the action by an outer automorphism preserves the underlying curve. Mirror reversal preserves its spectrum and gonality.

**Proposition 2.1 (P+X).** These curves have genus 136, the minimum faithful genus of $A_7$.

*Proof.* Riemann--Hurwitz gives
$$2g-2=2520(1-1/2-1/4-1/7)=270.$$
Minimality is a finite signature argument. Below 136 only hyperbolic triangle signatures with orders in $\{2,\ldots,7\}$ can occur. Test each signature first on the natural seven-sheeted quotient and then enumerate generating triples. None below 136 generates $A_7$. Four or more branch points and positive quotient genus do not supply smaller examples. The exhaustive calculation is `verify_accessory60.py`; random subgroup samples in that script are supplementary checks, not the classification proof. $\square$

**Lemma 2.2 (P).** Over a branch fibre $G/\langle x\rangle$, an element $h$ fixes
$$\frac{|C_G(h)|\,|h^G\cap\langle x\rangle|}{|x|}$$
points.

*Proof.* A coset $g\langle x\rangle$ is fixed exactly when $g^{-1}hg\in\langle x\rangle$. Each conjugate in this intersection has $|C_G(h)|$ conjugators. Divide by $|x|$. $\square$

There are 105 involutions with centralizer order 24, 630 elements of order four with centralizer order four, and two classes of 360 seven-cycles with centralizer order seven. The lemma gives:

| Element order | 2 | 4 | 7 | 3, 5, 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed points on $C$ | 18 | 2 | 3 | 0 |

In particular $g(D)=64$, since $270=2(2g(D)-2)+18$. Point stabilizers on a smooth curve in characteristic zero are cyclic: their derivative representation is faithful, and embeds them in $\mathbb C^*$. Distinct commuting involutions therefore have disjoint fixed sets.

## 2.2 The centralizer quotient

Put $H=C_G(\tau)/\langle\tau\rangle\simeq C_2\times S_3$, of order 12. There are four involutions of $H$ with 18 fixed points on $D$, called good, and three with two fixed points, called bad. The good involutions are images of involutions commuting with $\tau$; the bad ones lift to elements of order four whose square is $\tau$. Orders three and six act freely. One good involution is central.

These counts follow from
$$\#\operatorname{Fix}_{C/K}(\bar w)=\frac1{|K|}\sum_{k\in wK}\#\operatorname{Fix}_C(k)$$
when $w$ normalizes $K$ and $\bar w$ is its induced quotient action. Riemann--Hurwitz gives genus 28 for a good involution quotient and 32 for a bad one; $g(D/C_3)=22$, the quotient by the $S_3$ generated by noncentral good involutions has genus seven, and $g(T_0)=3$. The 16 subgroups of $H$ have quotient genera among $64,28,32,22,12,10,11,7,3$.

**Theorem 2.3 (P).** $\operatorname{gon}(D)\ge9$.

*Proof.* For a pencil of degree $d\le8$ and a good involution $v$, a birational product with $D\to D/v$ would give $64\le2\cdot28+d-1$, impossible. As the quotient has degree two, any common factor forces the pencil to descend through $D/v$. The four good involutions generate $H$, so the pencil descends through $D/H$ and $12\mid d$, also impossible. $\square$

## 2.3 The equality case at degree nine

**Theorem 2.4 (P, with the classical ruling theorem).** If $D$ has gonality nine, the compositum $F$ of its $H$-conjugate pencil fields has index $9,3$ or $1$ in $\mathbb C(D)$. The respective possibilities are:

1. One $H$-invariant pencil, with faithful dihedral target action of order 12. The curve is the normalized pullback of $\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^1/H$ by a degree-nine map $T_0\to\mathbb P^1$.
2. A degree-three map to a genus-four curve $Y$ carrying a faithful $H$-action of signature $(2,2,2,2,2)$. The curve is the normalized pullback of $Y\to Y/H\simeq\mathbb P^1$ by a degree-three map from $T_0$.
3. A smooth $(9,9)$ model in $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, with the good involutions exchanging its two gonal rulings.

In every case, the branch points on $T_0$ consist of three central-good points $t_i$, nine noncentral-good points $g_j$ and one bad point, and
$$\sum_{j=1}^9g_j\sim3\sum_{i=1}^3t_i.\tag{2.1}$$

*Proof.* Two distinct pencil fields have compositum index one or three, since the index properly divides nine. At index one, Castelnuovo--Severi is equality, $64=(9-1)^2$, so the image is smooth; the classical theorem for a smooth balanced quadric curve identifies its minimal pencils with its rulings. At index three, the intermediate curve has two degree-three pencils, hence genus at most four. Adding a conjugate outside its field would give $64\le3\cdot4+2\cdot8=28$, so this field is the full orbit compositum.

In the index-nine case the target-action kernel has order dividing nine. A kernel $C_3$ would yield a degree-three pencil on the genus-22 quotient, compared with its involution quotient of genus seven; Castelnuovo--Severi gives $22\le14+2$, impossible. The target action is faithful. In the index-three case a nontrivial kernel would again identify the intermediate curve with the genus-22 quotient. Galois correspondence now supplies the two pullbacks. A good involution preserving both rulings of the smooth quadric model has at most four fixed points unless it fixes a ruling map; both alternatives contradict its 18 fixed points and odd degree nine. Thus it exchanges the rulings.

The fixed divisors give 13 branch points, all of order two: $18/6=3$, $3\cdot18/6=9$, and $3\cdot2/6=1$. In the dihedral pullback, the order-six branch fibre must be $3\sum t_i$, whereas a good-reflection fibre is $\sum g_j$; comparison of fibres gives (2.1). In the genus-four pullback, Riemann--Hurwitz forces exactly one central, three good and one bad branch point, and comparison of the corresponding degree-three fibres gives the same relation. In the quadric model the central and the three other good fixed curves are $(1,1)$ graphs. The ratio of the product of the latter three equations to the cube of the former has a square descending to $T_0$. Its divisor yields (2.1). $\square$

**Example 2.5 (P+X).** A smooth $(9,9)$ curve with the same $H$ fixed-point data can have gonality nine. Choose the integral coefficient array of `model99.py`, supported on $i+j\equiv0\pmod3$ and symmetric under exchange and simultaneous reversal. This realizes the diagonal $S_3$ action and the factor swap. At $p=1000003$, exact elimination proves smoothness, the two fixed-graph restrictions are squarefree of degree 18, the bad fixed-point count is two, and orders three and six act freely. The corner coefficients let the same affine smoothness test cover all charts by symmetry. These finite-field nonvanishing certificates also hold in characteristic zero. A ruling has degree nine, and Theorem 2.3 gives the reverse bound.

Thus the local centralizer data, including the corresponding holomorphic Lefschetz character, cannot alone exclude degree nine on the actual quotient.

## 2.4 Saturated determinants

**Lemma 2.6 (P).** Let $f:X\to\mathbb P^1$ have degree $d$, with defining sections $s_0,s_1$ of $L=f^*\mathcal O(1)$, and let $v$ be an involution. The determinant
$$s_0\otimes v^*s_1-s_1\otimes v^*s_0\in H^0(X,L\otimes v^*L)$$
vanishes at every fixed point of $v$. It vanishes identically exactly when $f\circ v=f$. If $v$ has more than $2d$ fixed points, invariance follows. If it has exactly $2d$ and the determinant is nonzero, then
$$[L]+[v^*L]=[R_v].$$

*Proof.* Evaluate at a fixed point and use the proportionality of the two section vectors there. A nonzero section has zero divisor of degree $2d$. Equality exhausts that divisor. $\square$

**Theorem 2.7 (ramification transport; P+X).** $\operatorname{gon}(D)\ge10$.

*Proof.* Pull a hypothetical nine-pencil back to a $\tau$-invariant degree-18 map on $C$, with class $L$. For each involution $\mu\ne\tau$ commuting with $\tau$, its determinant vanishes on the 36 distinct points $R_\mu+R_{\tau\mu}$. It cannot be identically zero, because invariance under the Klein group would force $4\mid18$. Hence
$$[L]+[\mu^*L]=[R_\mu+R_{\tau\mu}].$$
Set $M=\sum_{h\in C_G(\tau)}[h^*L]$. Summing gives the same class
$$U:=24[R_\tau]+2M=24[R_\tau+R_\mu+R_{\tau\mu}]$$
for every Klein four-group through $\tau$. Its degree is $24\cdot54=1296$. The normalizers of
$$\{1,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\},\quad
\{1,(12)(34),(12)(56),(34)(56)\}$$
preserve $U$ and generate $A_7$. One sees the generation from the alternating groups on $1234$ and $3456$, which generate $A_6$ on $123456$, together with $(567)$. Thus $U$ is $A_7$-invariant.

An invariant bundle for a cyclic action can be linearized: rescale a chosen generator isomorphism to make its full iterate the identity. A free cyclic action then permits descent, forcing degree divisible by its order. Here $C_5$ acts freely, whereas $5\nmid1296$. This contradiction excludes nine. No division in the Picard group occurs, so torsion does not affect the argument. $\square$

## 2.5 Incidence and residual transport

The involution commuting graph of $A_7$ has 105 vertices, degree eight, 420 edges and 140 Klein triangles. Exact enumeration in `side_checks.py` verifies connectivity and normalizer generation. Saturation provides equal Picard classes along its triangles; a centralizer sum preserves a detecting prime that a sum over the whole group would remove.

For a broader involution class with $r$ fixed points and centralizer size $c$, a connected commuting graph and a quotient pencil of degree $r/2$ give a transported invariant class of degree $3cr$, provided the determinants are nonzero and the products stay in the class. Connectivity is a hypothesis. For $A_5$ with signature $(2,2,2,3)$ the analogous graph has five components, so no global invariant class follows merely from local identities.

Above saturation, a $\tau$-invariant degree-$m$ map gives instead
$$[L_\tau]+[\mu^*L_\tau]=[R_\mu+R_{\tau\mu}+E_{\tau,\mu}],\qquad
\deg E_{\tau,\mu}=2(m-r).$$
The residual divisors are effective. Differences of transported classes lie in $\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)$ and involve the differences of these residuals. A graph-cycle identity alone does not prove their balance. A general obstruction beyond saturation remains open.

The historical $\mathrm{PSL}_2(13)$ example uses $r=6$, $c=12$ and three $A_4$ normalizers through an involution that generate the group. A quotient three-pencil would force invariant degree $3cr=216$, incompatible with the free order-13 action. Reflection quotients of genus two supply degree-four pencils, giving gonality four for its genus-six involution quotients. The finite group input for this example is retained in the historical manuscripts rather than supplied by the present $A_7$ programs.
